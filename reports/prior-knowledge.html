Why the chronology matters

Earlier litigation positions and judicial rulings used descriptions such as unsupported, speculative, frivolous, duplicative, or made in bad faith. The chronology permits a document-specific comparison of what evidence was available, when it was served or filed, what later representations were made, and whether the record was corrected after additional notice.

The strongest sequence is the SEC's same-case filing: SEC counsel placed an investigative description on the 01637 docket seven days before defending the Answer. The Green materials establish docketing, service, and subsequent sworn file review. The FOMB sequence establishes that FOMB knew of PREPA Dkt. 5836 because it cited that entry, and that it later received three written requests concerning the Curtin record and the requested First Circuit correction.

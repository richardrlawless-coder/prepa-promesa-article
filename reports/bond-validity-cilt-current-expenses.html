PREPA Bond Validity, CILT, Current Expenses, and Settlement Risk

A consolidated review of the accounting, contractual, disclosure, debt-validity, creditor-priority, and restructuring questions developed in five existing reports.

Executive synthesis. The assembled record identifies a connected sequence of issues: PREPA included municipal electricity billings historically not collected in cash in reported Revenues and Net Revenues; those amounts were offset against Contributions in Lieu of Taxes (CILT); the resulting figures supported debt-service coverage presented to investors; and the classification of CILT and PPOA obligations as Current Expenses may materially affect both bondholder rights and the current settlement framework. Taken together, the evidence supports a focused merits review of disclosure accuracy, Trust Agreement compliance, bond-issuance assumptions, creditor priority, and government knowledge.

1. Municipal billings, CILT, and reported Net Revenues

The 2010 Official Statement is the clearest documentary starting point. It disclosed that PREPA calculated Trust Agreement Revenues, Current Expenses, and Net Revenues on an accrual basis. It also disclosed that those figures included electricity billings to municipalities that PREPA historically had not collected because the billings were offset against PREPA's CILT obligations.

The report identifies approximately $224.7 million in municipal-consumption and subsidy revenue for FY2009—approximately 35.7% of the reported Net Revenues used in the reconstruction. PREPA also reported approximately $1.237 billion in total receivables, approximately $471.4 million attributable to government agencies and municipalities, and an allowance for doubtful accounts of approximately $163.6 million.

The central question is not merely whether accrual accounting was permitted. It is whether the offering materials, read as a whole, accurately communicated the economic limitations of revenue that was historically not collected in cash and was tied to a CILT offset mechanism.

2. Trust Agreement treatment and debt-service coverage

The Trust Agreement defines Net Revenues as the excess of Revenues over Current Expenses. The CILT analysis therefore asks a threshold contractual question: whether CILT was an expense required by law or otherwise properly included among the costs paid before Net Revenues became available for debt service.

The FY2009 reconstruction illustrates the potential materiality. Reported Net Revenues of $629.496 million divided by debt service of $435.042 million produced reported coverage of 1.45x. Subtracting the disclosed $224.7 million municipal-and-subsidy component produces adjusted Net Revenues of approximately $404.8 million and estimated coverage of approximately 0.93x. The reports identify this as a forensic reconstruction; the earlier-year adjusted figures are estimates requiring validation against the complete underlying ledgers and offering workpapers.

If validated, the issue could affect compliance with coverage covenants, conditions supporting additional bond issuances, the accuracy and completeness of offering disclosures, and assumptions presently used to value bondholder claims.

3. Potential legal and regulatory issues identified for review

The reports organize the evidence for investigation under several related theories:

  • Materially misleading disclosure or omission: whether including historically uncollected municipal billings in coverage metrics, without fully incorporating their economic limitations, created a misleading impression of debt-service capacity.
  • Fraudulent inducement or misleading half-truth: whether technically accurate individual disclosures nevertheless presented an incomplete overall picture to purchasers of PREPA bonds.
  • Trust Agreement noncompliance: whether CILT, Current Expenses, Net Revenues, or coverage calculations were applied inconsistently with the governing contract.
  • Bond-issuance validity and authorization: whether particular issuances satisfied contractual and financial conditions applicable to additional debt.
  • Debt-dependent financing: whether repeated borrowing, refinancing, and capitalized interest masked an inability to amortize prior debt from operations.
  • Title III claim-priority and disclosure questions: whether the restructuring record and settlement assumptions fully account for known debt-validity, Current Expense, and creditor-priority evidence.

These are investigative and merits-review questions. The reports identify the evidence, calculations, and records that permit each theory to be tested against offering workpapers, ledgers, certifications, internal communications, and sworn testimony.

4. PPOA obligations as Current Expenses

A separate but connected Current Expense question concerns renewable Power Purchase and Operating Agreements. The Blue Beetle III materials identify the facility as “must run,” bar curtailment for economic reasons, provide for payments following qualifying curtailment, and integrate the project into continuing grid operations.

Those characteristics support review of whether PPOA payment obligations function as purchased-power or system-operating costs. If they qualify as Current Expenses, the payment waterfall and the amount of revenue legally available for bondholder distributions may differ materially from the assumptions used in the current settlement framework.

5. Consequences for the PREPA settlement framework

The combined reports identify several potential restructuring consequences: reclassification of PPOA obligations, recalculation of revenues available for debt service, reassessment of bondholder claim strength, changes to unsecured recovery modeling, establishment of reserves, targeted discovery, and negotiated adjustments to creditor treatment.

The significance is cumulative. CILT treatment affects Net Revenues; Net Revenues affect coverage; coverage affects disclosure and issuance assumptions; Current Expense classification affects payment priority; and each of those questions can affect the allocation of value among creditor groups.

6. Federal investigative and notice record

The related record includes SEC subpoena activity concerning PREPA's financial condition, debt-service capability, receivables, subsidies, solvency, continuing disclosure, and support for offering materials. Later FOIA productions and government records have been cited as evidence that federal agencies possessed substantial PREPA-related investigative material while the underlying bond-validity and priority questions remained unresolved.

That record supports targeted inquiry into what government officials, investigators, underwriters, fiscal agents, restructuring professionals, and other participants knew; when they knew it; what was presented to investors, Congress, and the courts; and whether later representations accurately reflected the available evidence.

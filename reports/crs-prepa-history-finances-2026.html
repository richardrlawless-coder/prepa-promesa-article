Congressional Research Service Report R49489: PREPA History and Finances

Puerto Rico Electric Power Authority (PREPA): History and Finances, D. Andrew Austin, Congressional Research Service, October 20, 2026. This 49-page PDF is a nonpartisan overview of PREPA's finances, operations, governance, and restructuring litigation.

Read the original CRS report (PDF) · Alternative full-text edition

Key passages and document references

  1. CILT, municipal receivables and unbilled electricity — report p. 37 (PDF p. 41). CRS describes electricity supplied to municipalities and government offices that PREPA did not bill, partly in connection with payments in lieu of taxation, and reports that approximately 14% of power generated in 2013 was lost or unaccounted for. This raises questions concerning revenue treatment and accounting; it does not prove that the 1974 Trust Agreement was violated.
  2. Financial distress and contemporaneous bond disclosures — report p. 8 (PDF p. 12). By May 2014 PREPA relied on capital reserve funds for fuel purchases, while a June 2014 government report raised substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern. Such information warrants comparison with contemporaneous 2013 bond offering documents, but does not itself establish fraudulent issuance.
  3. Disputed net-revenue calculations — report pp. 28–29 (PDF pp. 32–33). CRS summarizes the continuing legal dispute concerning PREPA net revenues and the court-authorized accounting counterclaim, without determining any particular creditor's entitlement.
  4. Renewable project development — report pp. 25, 37 (PDF pp. 29, 41). CRS recounts a Puerto Rico Energy Bureau warning against PREPA interference with or delay of solar and other renewable resource procurement. This gives institutional context but is not an adjudication of the Blue Beetle PPOA.
  5. Governance and project management — report pp. 10, 39 (PDF pp. 14, 43). CRS cites earlier regulatory criticism of expensive project initiation and termination as well as congressional testimony discussing political or electoral influences on PREPA strategic decisions.

How to interpret this report

The CRS report provides independent background and cites underlying records relevant to PREPA's financial condition, rate setting, revenue practices and energy planning. It does not evaluate SEC Matter B-02949, conclude that particular lawyers made false statements, determine that PREPA bonds were fraudulently issued, or establish a criminal investigation. For legal proceedings, readers should consult the cited financial statements, regulatory documents, and court orders alongside the CRS discussion.

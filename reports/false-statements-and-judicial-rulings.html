WHAT THE SUE CURTIN FOIA DOCUMENTS ESTABLISH AND WHY THE FALSE STATEMENTS WERE MATERIAL

The Sue Curtin FOIA production materially changes the factual record concerning Richard R. Lawless’s relationship with the Securities and Exchange Commission, the SEC’s PREPA investigation, and communications between SEC and Department of Justice personnel.

The importance of these documents is that they are not Mr. Lawless’s descriptions of events. They are contemporaneous Government records created, maintained, routed, and preserved within Government investigative systems.

1. The SEC Maintained an Identifiable PREPA Investigative Matter

The records identify PREPA Matter B-02949 and associate Mr. Lawless’s submissions with that matter.

The SEC’s own records describe Enforcement personnel analyzing Mr. Lawless’s TCR in connection with the PREPA investigation, identify an investigative work item associated with his TCR, document investigative strategy and attorney calls, and show his materials being routed through investigative-document-management systems.

This establishes substantially more than the passive receipt of unsolicited correspondence.

2. The SEC Internally Treated Mr. Lawless as a Whistleblower

Internal SEC records identify “WB_Lawless, Richard” and associate his materials with whistleblower processing in PREPA Matter B-02949.

Other SEC records expressly describe materials as “WHISTLEBLOWER PRODUCTION” and state:

“Production made by whistleblower on 1/24/2017 in PREPA matter.”

These were SEC-created classifications, not labels assigned by Mr. Lawless. They materially differ from the litigation characterization that Mr. Lawless merely submitted a TCR and “claimed whistleblower status.”

3. Government Personnel Requested and Used Mr. Lawless’s Analysis

The records document reciprocal substantive interaction.

Government personnel requested Mr. Lawless’s analysis of PREPA’s debt-service coverage and representations concerning material defaults. The resulting work was routed into SEC investigative systems.

The records also document direct telephone communications, including an SEC entry captioned “call from whistleblower.”

Thus, the Government’s own records show requests for substantive work, direct communications, investigative routing, and preservation of Mr. Lawless’s analysis.

4. The Records Establish SEC-DOJ Coordination

The Curtin production documents communications between SEC personnel and DOJ Antitrust Trial Attorney Samson Asiyanbi concerning Mr. Lawless’s PREPA complaint.

SEC personnel circulated and preserved those communications and arranged follow-up discussions. After communicating with SEC personnel, the DOJ attorney stated that he would keep them informed concerning “our review.”

Another record documents DOJ follow-up with SEC personnel after a direct conversation with Mr. Lawless.

These records do not establish DOJ’s ultimate conclusions. They do establish that SEC-DOJ engagement concerning Mr. Lawless’s PREPA evidence was real and documented within Government records.

5. The Records Materially Affect the Prior “No Evidence” Narrative

Across the related litigation, aspects of Mr. Lawless’s account were repeatedly characterized as unsupported, speculative, implausible, conspiratorial, repetitive, or lacking objective governmental corroboration.

The Curtin records establish objective Government evidence of:

  • PREPA Matter B-02949;

  • SEC analysis of Mr. Lawless’s TCR;

  • internal whistleblower processing;

  • investigative work items and document routing;

  • attorney communications with Mr. Lawless;

  • Government requests for his financial analysis;

  • preservation of his work as investigative material; and

  • documented SEC-DOJ communications and review.

The records do not establish every legal allegation advanced by Mr. Lawless. They do establish that significant portions of the underlying factual account had contemporaneous corroboration within the Government’s own files.

6. The Conflict Is Particularly Significant in Case No. 5:21-cv-01637

In the FOIA litigation, the SEC denied material portions of Mr. Lawless’s alleged investigative and working relationship and later argued that he had provided no admissible evidence showing that the SEC had made false or improper statements.

The Court subsequently characterized the exchanges as “largely one-sided,” found that they did not establish a working relationship, described the available documents as “anodyne,” and concluded that the evidence overwhelmingly supported the SEC.

The Court specifically regarded the absence of corroborating documents as particularly damaging to Mr. Lawless’s position.

The Curtin production later supplied precisely that missing category of Government-generated corroboration: investigative processing, whistleblower classifications, attorney calls, substantive requests for analysis, investigative routing, and SEC-DOJ communications.

An additional discrepancy concerns the SEC’s FOIA searches. The SEC initially reported no responsive records for a request involving Chairman Gensler emails concerning Mr. Lawless. It subsequently identified approximately 330 email records and produced 106 responsive pages.

These developments do not automatically invalidate the Court’s rulings, but they materially change the factual record upon which the SEC’s representations and Mr. Lawless’s credibility were evaluated.

7. The Effect Extended Beyond a Single Case

The significance of the incomplete factual record became cumulative.

Government filings repeatedly characterized the underlying allegations as unsupported, speculative, repetitive, conspiratorial, or frivolous. Courts adopted or relied upon portions of those descriptions. Those decisions then became part of Mr. Lawless’s litigation history and were relied upon in subsequent proceedings.

Eventually, the accumulated history contributed to findings of frivolousness, a vexatious-litigant order, filing restrictions, and appellate dispositions.

The resulting sequence is significant:

Government-controlled corroborating evidence was absent when the allegations were initially characterized as unsupported; rulings entered without that evidence became support for later frivolous and repetitive-litigation characterizations; and those resulting judicial labels were then used in subsequent proceedings before the Curtin records became available.

Conclusion

The Curtin FOIA documents establish that the SEC maintained PREPA Matter B-02949, processed Mr. Lawless’s information within investigative and whistleblower systems, analyzed his TCR, requested and preserved substantive financial work, communicated directly with him, and coordinated with DOJ concerning his PREPA complaint.

The documents do not establish every allegation of wrongdoing or automatically overcome procedural or sovereign-immunity defenses.

They do establish a narrower and potentially consequential point:

The Government possessed contemporaneous records materially corroborating factual matters that were repeatedly characterized in litigation as unsupported, self-proclaimed, speculative, or conspiratorial. Those characterizations entered judicial decisions and subsequently became part of the basis for additional adverse rulings.

The Curtin production therefore materially changes the factual record against which the Government’s prior representations and the judicial rulings that relied upon them can now be evaluated.

These false statements leave little question of their material nature because the exact government wording and the governments false arguments appear in the judicial rulings.
